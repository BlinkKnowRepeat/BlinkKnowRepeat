India’s rate corridor, explained: what RBI’s own daily data shows

The RBI’s 19 November money-market data shows the overnight rate at 5.56%, with the standing-deposit rate at 5.25% and the marginal-standing rate at 5.75%. That corridor points to a 5.50% policy repo rate. The next decision is reportedly due 4 December.

Published Nov 20, 2026, 10:00 UTC · 4 min · Reported by third parties

In a blink

  • RBI’s 19 Nov 2026 data: overnight weighted-average rate 5.56%, SDF 5.25%, MSF 5.75%.
  • The corridor is the repo rate ±0.25 percentage point, which implies a 5.50% repo rate.
  • Retail inflation for October was reported at 6.09%, above the 6% upper tolerance band; the next policy decision is reportedly 4 December.

Why it matters

The repo rate sets the cost of money in the economy. It feeds into home-loan and car-loan EMIs, fixed-deposit rates and, indirectly, the rupee and gold prices.

Reading the RBI’s daily table

Each working day the RBI publishes money-market operations. The one dated 19 November 2026 (press release 2026-2027/1527) shows the overnight weighted average rate at 5.56%, call money at 5.59% and triparty repo at 5.55%. It lists the Standing Deposit Facility at 5.25% and the Marginal Standing Facility at 5.75%.

Why the corridor tells you the repo rate

Under the RBI’s framework the SDF rate sits 25 basis points below the repo rate and the MSF rate 25 basis points above it. A corridor of 5.25%–5.75% therefore implies a policy repo rate of 5.50%. This is our inference from the framework; the RBI’s policy statement is the authoritative source for the decision itself.

What third-party reports add

News reports say the RBI raised the rate from 5.25% on 7 October, its first increase in 44 months, that October retail inflation was 6.09% (up from 5.54%), and that the next Monetary Policy Committee decision is due 4 December with a further 25 basis point rise widely expected. We have not confirmed these against RBI or MoSPI pages.

What it means for you

If the repo rate rises, floating-rate loans linked to it become costlier and new fixed-deposit rates tend to move up. Savers and borrowers feel it with a lag that depends on the bank and the loan contract.

What we could not confirm. Only the corridor figures come from an RBI page we read directly. The October rate hike, the 6.09% inflation figure and the 4 December date are third-party reports and should be checked against the RBI and MoSPI sites.
