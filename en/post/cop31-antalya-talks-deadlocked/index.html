COP31 in Antalya: finance and fossil-fuel phase-out kept talks stuck

The UN climate conference ran 9–20 November in Türkiye. UN News reported ministers deadlocked on climate finance, the pace of moving away from fossil fuels and what counts as a “just” transition. We could not confirm the final outcome.

Published Nov 19, 2026, 12:00 UTC · 4 min · Primary source

In a blink

  • COP31 was scheduled for 9–20 November 2026 at the Antalya Expo Center, Türkiye.
  • Murat Kurum is COP31 President; Australia’s Chris Bowen is President of Negotiations.
  • By 18 November UN News described ministers as deadlocked on finance, fossil-fuel phase-out and “just transition”.

Why it matters

COP outcomes shape how much money flows to climate adaptation, how fast countries cut emissions and who pays. For India, climate finance and a fair transition from coal are central issues.

Who hosts and who leads

The UNFCCC lists Türkiye as host, with Murat Kurum as COP31 President. Under an arrangement accepted at COP30, Australia’s Chris Bowen is President of Negotiations. Pre-COP meetings were held in Fiji and Tuvalu.

The sticking points

UN News coverage dated 18 November describes ministers as deadlocked on three questions: how much climate finance wealthier countries provide, how quickly the world phases down fossil fuels, and how to define and deliver a just transition for workers and communities.

Why consensus is hard

Climate COP decisions are normally taken by consensus among nearly 200 parties. A single unresolved bracket in the draft text can hold up the whole package, which is why final days often run past the scheduled close.

What to watch

The final decision texts will be published on the UNFCCC website. Until you have read them, treat any summary of “what COP31 agreed” (including this one) as provisional.

What we could not confirm. The conference was scheduled to close on 20 November. We found no confirmed final outcome at the time of writing, so this post covers the talks up to 18 November only.

How a COP decision gets made

Negotiators write draft text. Unresolved choices appear in square brackets.

Parties trade positions in working groups and ministerial consultations.

The presidency tries to find language nobody blocks. One objection can stop a decision.

The final plenary adopts the decision texts, often after the scheduled closing time.
