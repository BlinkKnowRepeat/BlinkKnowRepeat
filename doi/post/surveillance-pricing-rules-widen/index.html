Shops, prices and your data: the rules on personalised pricing are widening

US regulators and states are acting on “surveillance pricing”. Retailers say algorithmic pricing is an old practice with new tools.

प्रकाशित। 2026-11-22 17:00 UTC · 4 मिन · त्रिया पक्षें द्वारा रिपोर्ट कीता गेआ

मशीन-अनुवादत संस्करण

इक पलक च

  • The FTC moved in August to require retailers to disclose personalised pricing.[2]
  • Maryland’s ban on surveillance pricing in food retail took effect on 1 October 2026.[3]
  • New Jersey’s Fair Price Protection Act was signed on 23 July, and in-scope retailers have until 1 August 2027 to comply.[3]

एह् की म्हत्तवपूर्ण ऐ?

If a shop can set a price from what it knows about you, two shoppers can pay different amounts for the same item. The new rules aim to make that visible.

Why people are worried

Coverage on 18 October says terms such as “dynamic pricing” and “surveillance pricing” are causing anxiety online. The National Retail Federation says algorithmic pricing is a new way of doing what retailers have done for years.[1]

What experts say about groceries

One expert noted that changing prices through the day, as airlines and ride apps do, is exceedingly rare in grocery and general merchandise today.[1]

What regulators are doing

The FTC moved in August to require disclosure of personalised pricing, and a Senate Judiciary subcommittee held a hearing on 4 August 2026.[2] At least 30 states have considered pricing bills this year.[3]

The company view

Instacart said it ended its pricing tests in December 2025 and disputes describing them as surveillance pricing.[1]

जिंʼदी असीं पुश्टी नेईं करी सके। This is US coverage. We have not read the bills or the FTC action ourselves, and rules differ by state.
